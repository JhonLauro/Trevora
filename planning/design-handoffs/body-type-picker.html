Trevora · Ink · design brief

Body type picker

Improve how an owner tells us what shape their vehicle is. This page is the context — it renders the current design from the real source, states the constraints that cannot move, and ends with the prompt to work from.

1. What this is and where it appears

Trevora is a vehicle service-history system for owners in the Philippines. Body type is asked once, when a vehicle is added, on two screens:

  • RegisterVehiclePage — step 2 of signup, immediately after account creation. A brand-new user's third screen in a row that is a form.
  • AddVehiclePage — adding a second or later vehicle from the Garage.

It is usually pre-filled. Picking a model from the catalogue sets the body type automatically, and the hint changes to "Filled in from the model. Change it if it is wrong." The picker is doing its hardest work for the minority of owners whose model is not in the catalogue — which is exactly the person least likely to know the vocabulary.

It is a required field. Downstream, bodyType selects the silhouette on the parts map, and its vehicleClass (car or motorcycle) decides which components a vehicle is shown at all — a motorcycle is never offered an aircon, a car never a drive chain. A wrong answer here is visible later, so being easy to answer correctly matters more than being fast to skip.

3. What is wrong with it

Stated plainly, in rough order of how much it matters:

  1. Pairs collapse at 72px. Sedan against hatchback, and MPV against van, are close to indistinguishable at rendered size. The source file admits this: the shapes were deliberately exaggerated because "real proportions would make several of these near-identical", and the CSS comment concedes the examples "do the real work for anyone who does not know the term". The drawing is currently the weakest of the three signals, when it should be the fastest.
  2. The glyphs are flat single-colour fills. No window line, no wheel arch, no interior detail. The only things carrying the difference are the outline and the wheel radius, which is very little information for seven categories.
  3. Six car options in a two-column grid is three rows, and one column on mobile is a six-item scroll — inside a signup flow already flagged internally as too long.
  4. The "Two wheels" group holds exactly one option, so it renders as a single card under its own heading and looks unbalanced against the six above it.
  5. Selection is quiet. A border, an inset shadow, and the glyph shifting from --ink-muted to --ink. On a page of white cards it is easy to miss which one is chosen.
  6. The radio dot competes with the glyph for the role of "the control", and takes the leading edge of every row.

4. Constraints that cannot move

Ink reserves all chroma for record status

The product is greyscale on warm paper. The only saturated colour anywhere is the status ramp, and it means one specific thing: whether a service record has been checked by a human. Do not introduce an accent colour, a brand colour, or a coloured selected state. Distinction has to come from structure, weight, size, spacing and motion.

--paper #f7f4ef · --surface #ffffff · --ink #1c1b19 · --ink-muted #57534c · --ink-faint #8b857c · --rule #d8d1c5 · --sunken #efede8

Keep all three signals: shape, description, examples

They fail in different ways and cover for each other. The silhouette is fastest when it is clear; the description rescues the pairs that look alike; the examples settle MPV against van, which neither of the other two reliably does. A screen reader gets nothing from the drawing and reads the description instead. Removing any one of them to save space is a regression, even if the redesigned shapes are much better.

Also fixed

  • Real radio inputs. Single choice in a form — radios bring arrow-key navigation and group semantics for free. A grid of <button>s would have to re-implement both. The visual treatment of the radio is open; the semantics are not.
  • Type floor: 16px body, 15px for secondary text. Nothing smaller.
  • Touch targets ≥ 44px. Must work at 360px wide.
  • The seven categories are fixed — sedan, hatchback, SUV, MPV, pickup, van, motorcycle — and so are their ids, labels, descriptions and examples. They are a database check constraint, not a design choice.
  • Glyphs are drawn in currentColor so they inherit the selected state without a palette of their own. Keep that.

5. Explicitly out of scope

This is not the parts map. A separate and much larger set of drawings (vehicleShapes.js) renders four views per vehicle — side, front, rear, engine bay — with numbered component markers on them. Those were rebuilt recently and are settled. They share nothing with these glyphs: different viewBox, different level of detail, different job, and they carry anchor points these do not. Do not try to unify them.

The make and model fields above this one are also out of scope, though there is a known intention to turn make into a tappable grid of common Philippine marques. If your layout would conflict with that, say so rather than designing around it silently.

6. What "better" means

Judge any proposal against these, in this order:

  1. Someone who does not know the word "MPV" still answers correctly.
  2. Sedan vs hatchback, and MPV vs van, are tellable apart at final rendered size, not just when enlarged.
  3. The whole control is shorter or feels shorter than three rows plus an orphan.
  4. The chosen option is unmistakable at a glance.
  5. It survives 360px width and a screen reader.
  6. No new colour.

7. What to hand back

  • A layout for the control at desktop and at 360px — including how the single motorcycle option sits alongside six car options without looking like a mistake.
  • Seven redrawn silhouettes. Keep the 0 0 76 40 viewBox and single-path-per-shape structure if you can, since swapping them is then a paths-only change; if a redesign needs a different box or a second path per shape, that is allowed — say so.
  • The selected, hover and keyboard-focus states.
  • A note on anything above you disagree with. The constraints in §4 are real, but the problems in §3 are one person's reading and worth challenging.

8. The prompt

Copy from here down, with this page attached or open alongside.

Redesign the body type picker for Trevora, a vehicle service-history
product for owners in the Philippines. The attached page renders the
current design from the live source and states the constraints.

The control asks a new owner what shape their vehicle is: sedan,
hatchback, SUV, MPV, pickup, van, or motorcycle. It appears during
signup and when adding a vehicle. It is usually pre-filled from the
chosen model, so it matters most for owners whose model is not in our
catalogue — the people least likely to know the terminology.

The problem: each option shows a 72x38 silhouette, a one-line
description and three example models. At that size the silhouettes do
not carry their weight. Sedan and hatchback look alike; MPV and van
look alike. The drawings are flat single-colour fills with no window
line or wheel arch, so only the outline and wheel size distinguish
seven categories. Six car options in a two-column grid runs three rows,
and the lone motorcycle sits under its own heading looking orphaned.
Selection is a border and a subtle ink shift, easy to miss.

Hard constraints:
- The product is greyscale on warm paper (#f7f4ef). ALL saturated
  colour is reserved for service-record status and means something
  specific. No accent colour, no coloured selected state. Distinction
  must come from structure, weight, size, spacing and motion.
- Keep all three signals per option: silhouette, description, examples.
  They fail differently and cover for each other. A screen reader gets
  the description, not the drawing. Dropping one to save space is a
  regression.
- Real radio inputs (arrow-key navigation, group semantics). Visual
  treatment is open; the semantics are not.
- 16px body text, 15px floor. Touch targets 44px or larger. Must work
  at 360px wide.
- The seven categories and their labels, descriptions and examples are
  fixed by a database constraint.

Out of scope: the parts-map artwork, which is a separate and settled
set of four-view component diagrams. Do not unify the two.

Success, in order: someone who does not know "MPV" still answers
correctly; sedan/hatchback and MPV/van are tellable apart at rendered
size rather than only when enlarged; the control is shorter than three
rows plus an orphan; the selected option is unmistakable; it survives
360px and a screen reader; no new colour.

Give me the layout at desktop and 360px, seven redrawn silhouettes
(keep the 0 0 76 40 viewBox and one path per shape if you can, so
swapping them is a paths-only change), and the selected, hover and
focus states. Tell me where you disagree with the problem statement.
