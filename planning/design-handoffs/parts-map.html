Trevora · Ink · design brief

Parts map

Redraw the vehicle diagrams that carry component service history. This page renders all thirteen current drawings from the live source, states what they have to do, and ends with the prompt to work from.

1. What it is

Trevora consolidates a vehicle's service history for owners in the Philippines. On the Vehicle page, the Components tab answers "what parts of my car have actually been worked on?" — a diagram of the vehicle with a numbered marker on each component, over a list of the same components.

Each marker has exactly two states, and this is the whole point of the screen:

  • Has service records — something in this history mentions this part.
  • No record found — nothing does.

It is deliberately not a maintenance predictor. An earlier version graded parts as due/overdue against a hardcoded interval table; that was removed because the intervals were car conventions applied to every vehicle, and it told scooter riders their engine was fine when it was not. The diagram reports what happened. It never forecasts.

2. The four views

Thirteen drawings serve seven body types, because the views are shared unevenly:

ViewDrawn perMarkersCarries
Sidebody type — 7 drawings6 car / 7 bikelights, body, suspension, brakes, tires, exhaust
Frontvehicle class — 2 drawings5 / 4lights, engine, battery, cooling, brakes
Rearvehicle class — 2 drawings4 / 5lights, body, tires, exhaust
Engine bayvehicle class — 2 drawings7 / 6cooling, battery, air filter, engine, aircon, fluids, transmission

Why four views rather than one. Seven components live under a car's bonnet. On a single side profile they collide with the front wheel, or have to be relocated somewhere less true to escape it. Giving the crowded region its own canvas is what makes the engine bay legible at all.

Why side is per body type and the rest are shared. A pickup and an MPV differ in profile, and that is the view where the difference shows. Head-on and under the bonnet they were judged close enough that six near-identical drawings would be six places to drift out of sync rather than six pieces of information. This call is open to challenge — see §4.

4. What is wrong with them

These are my own drawings and this list is not generous. In rough order of severity:

  1. Front and rear cars are the same outline. Identical body path; only the details differ — headlamps and grille versus tail lamps, boot seam and tailpipe. Switching from Front to Rear shows the same silhouette twice, which makes the tab feel broken.
  2. The engine bay is boxes inside a box. It is legible and well spaced, but it reads as a wireframe rather than as an engine bay. Nothing about it looks like an engine.
  3. Every motorcycle is a scooter. One drawing serves the whole class, so an underbone or big-bike owner is shown a step-through with a leg shield.
  4. The shared views ignore body type entirely. A pickup owner gets their own side profile and then three generic car drawings — three of their four views are not their vehicle.
  5. Everything is one weight. Flat fill, uniform 3px stroke, no shading and no depth, so nothing recedes and no part of the drawing is emphasised over another.
  6. Side profiles separate mainly by outline. Sedan against hatchback is still close at the size the panel actually renders.
  7. Wheels are a circle with a smaller circle in it. No tread, no spokes, no arch — they read as washers.
  8. Orientation is never stated. Everything faces left; nothing tells the viewer that, and the front/rear views give no cue which end they are.

5. Constraints that cannot move

Chroma belongs to status and nothing else

The product is greyscale on warm paper. The only saturated colour is the status ramp, and on this screen it means one thing: whether a component has service records. The vehicle itself must stay entirely neutral — no body colour, no paint, no accent. If the drawing takes colour, the markers stop being the only thing that means something.

--paper #f7f4ef --sunken #efede8 --rule #d8d1c5 --ink-faint #8b857c --ink-muted #57534c --ink #1c1b19 --status-ok-text #285c33

Status is never colour alone

A documented component is a filled marker; an undocumented one is hollow. The difference has to survive greyscale, and the list underneath repeats every state in words. Keep both the fill/hollow distinction and the neutral-drawing rule.

Also fixed

  • Markers are decorative to assistive tech. The SVG is aria-hidden; the list beneath is the accessible path, because every marker duplicates a list row and putting the same controls in the tab order twice is worse than a pointer-only map. The view tabs are the only real buttons. Do not propose markers as the sole way to reach a component.
  • Markers must not collide. Radius 13 in each drawing's own viewBox; a script checks every anchor pair and fails under 30 units apart. Any redraw has to come with anchor coordinates that pass it.
  • The component list is fixed by a keyword taxonomy: cars get 13, motorcycles 12 (no aircon, no transmission; they get a drive chain/CVT and fairings instead). Every component must appear in at least one view. A component may appear in several — a car has lights at both ends.
  • The drawing is hidden below 720px, where a marker is smaller than a fingertip. The tabs stay as a filter over the list. Design for the desktop panel, roughly 640–800px wide.
  • A vehicle with no recorded body type gets no drawing at all, and says so. Never substitute a default silhouette — that asserts a fact the record does not carry.
  • Flat SVG, no raster. It ships as paths in a JS module.

6. Worth knowing

The parts map is not in the original project proposal. It is justified as part of "consolidating and presenting service history", but it is scope that was added, so it should earn its place rather than grow. A redesign that makes it dramatically more elaborate is probably the wrong direction.

Component attribution is currently guessed from receipt keywords on every render, not stored. So a marker can be wrong and the owner cannot correct it. That is a known gap being fixed separately — but it argues for a diagram that looks approximate rather than authoritative.

7. What "better" means

  1. Front and rear are unmistakably different views of the vehicle.
  2. The engine bay reads as an engine bay, not a wireframe.
  3. A pickup or van owner recognises their own vehicle across the views they are shown.
  4. Markers stay legible, uncrowded and clearly two-state.
  5. Motorcycles are not all scooters, or the drawing is generic enough not to claim otherwise.
  6. No colour on the vehicle. No forecasting, no due-dates, no third status.
  7. It still looks like the same product as the rest of Ink.

8. What to hand back

  • Redrawn views. Say clearly which are per body type and which are shared — if you think front/rear should be per body type after all, argue for it and accept the count.
  • Anchor coordinates per component per view, in the drawing's viewBox, at least 30 units apart.
  • Marker treatment for both states plus selected and hover.
  • Whether the four-view split is right at all. If one richer drawing could carry every component legibly, that is a better answer and I would rather hear it.
